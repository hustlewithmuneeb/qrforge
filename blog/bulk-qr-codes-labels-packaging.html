Bulk QR Codes for Labels and Packaging: A Practical Guide

By QRForge Team · 10 November 2026 · QRForge Blog

One QR code is a two-minute job. Fifty codes for a product range, each pointing somewhere different, is where one-by-one generation falls apart — and where mistakes creep in. Bulk generation flips the work around: you prepare one spreadsheet of destinations, and the tool produces every code from it, named and ready to print.

What bulk codes are good for

Anywhere a printed item needs its own destination: product labels linking to manuals, setup videos or warranty registration; reorder codes on packaging that take a customer straight back to the product page; asset and inventory tags for tools and equipment; table numbers that open the menu or an ordering page; event badges and passes. The pattern is always the same — many items, one destination each.

Step 1: Prepare the CSV

Your spreadsheet needs two columns: name and destination. The name is a label for you — it becomes the file name of the downloaded code, so make it meaningful ("Blue Mug — manual" beats "row 12"). The destination is the full link that code should open. Save or export the sheet as a CSV file. Before generating anything, click three or four of the links yourself: a bulk tool faithfully prints whatever is in the file, including the typo in row 34.

Step 2: Generate the batch

Open the QRForge bulk generator and upload the CSV. You will see a preview grid of every row — check the names against the destinations — then download all the PNGs in one go, or print the sheet directly if the codes are going onto labels you cut yourself. The browser-based generator handles up to 100 rows per batch; for longer lists, split the CSV into batches of 100 and keep the files together. Bulk generation is not counted against the guest 10-free-codes limit on QRForge.

Step 3: Print labels that actually scan

Labels are scanned up close, so codes can be smaller than posters — 2 cm wide is a workable minimum for a simple code on a flat label — but three rules still apply. Keep the quiet zone, the clear border, inside the label rather than trimmed off. Keep contrast high: black on white or a pale label colour; metallic and transparent labels are scan killers. And beware curved surfaces — a code wrapped tightly around a small bottle distorts at the edges, so go a size up or choose a flatter spot. Print and scan one label on the finished product before the full run; it is the cheapest quality check there is, and the built-in QR scanner can verify a design if you want a second opinion beyond your phone.

Step 4: Keep the master list

Save the CSV you generated from. It is the map between every printed code and its destination: when a manual link changes or a product is replaced, the sheet tells you exactly which labels are affected. Store it with the product's other documents, and keep the same naming discipline for the next batch — future you will be grateful. One more consideration: every code from the bulk tool is static, so its destination is permanent. If a campaign needs destinations you can change later, read the static vs dynamic comparison before printing thousands of anything. And if some of those labels belong in a café or restaurant, the menu QR guide covers the table side of the same job.

QRForge does not collect, upload or store your QR content, logos, images or CSV files on any server — all processing happens locally in your browser.
