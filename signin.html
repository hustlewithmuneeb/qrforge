Frequently asked questions

Straight answers — including the honest limits of a free static generator.

Are QRForge codes really free, with no watermark?

Yes. Generation and all four export formats (PNG, JPG, SVG, PDF) are free, with no watermark and no signup. The tool runs entirely in your browser.

Do these QR codes expire?

No. QRForge creates static QR codes: the content is encoded directly in the pattern, so the code works forever as long as it is printed and scannable. What can change is the destination itself — for example, if your website URL stops working, the QR still points to it.

Static vs dynamic QR codes — what is the difference?

A static QR encodes the final content (your URL, Wi-Fi password, contact). A dynamic QR encodes a short redirect link on a company's server, so the destination can be edited later and scans can be tracked. That requires a small redirect service on the internet. QRForge offers both: turn on Tracked QR in the studio for URL, PDF/Menu and Google Review codes. Tracked codes are registered in your panel straight away, but the redirect and scan counting only start once the site owner switches on the free cloud connection (a one-time setup) — until then, print static codes for anything urgent.

What size should I print a QR code?

At least 2 × 2 cm (about 0.8 in) for close-range scanning, and bigger the further away the scanner will be — a rough rule is print width ≈ scanning distance ÷ 10. Use the 2048px or 4096px export (or SVG) for large prints so modules stay sharp.

What is the quiet zone and why does it matter?

The quiet zone is the clear margin around the QR pattern. Scanners use it to find the code's edges. Keep at least 4 modules of empty space (QRForge defaults to 4) and never place text, logos or page edges inside it.

Can I put my logo in the middle?

Yes. QRForge embeds your logo in a white padded box and automatically raises error correction to H (~30%), which is designed to tolerate a covered centre. Keep the logo modest in size and always test-scan.

Why did my coloured QR stop scanning?

Usually low contrast: the QR colour is too light against the background. QRForge shows a live contrast ratio — aim for 4.5:1 or higher, and never invert a QR (light modules on a dark background) unless you have tested it, as many scanners struggle with inverted codes.

What do the frames do, and does the frame text export too?

The frames gallery adds a border or a labelled banner around your code — classic, double-line, rounded, corner brackets, black banners (top or bottom), a bordered label strip, a speech-bubble frame, or divider lines. Edit the frame text (or tap a preset like SCAN FOR MENU or LEAVE A REVIEW) and it is drawn into the live preview and into every export: PNG, JPG, true-vector SVG (the text is a real SVG text element) and PDF. Keep frame text short so it stays large and readable.

How do I know my QR still scans after adding a logo?

Press Test scan this QR next to the preview. QRForge re-decodes your current design — styles and logo included — right in your browser and tells you whether it reads back exactly. If it fails, raise the contrast, keep error correction on H, shrink or remove the logo, or switch back to square modules (the most decoder-friendly style). You can also upload any QR image in the studio's Upload & scan card to read it — and recreate it as your own design.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. QR generation, logo colour sampling, image scanning, CSV bulk generation and saved codes all run locally in your browser. Closing this page loses nothing except unsaved work; saved codes live in your browser's localStorage.

How does the bulk generator work?

Upload a CSV with the headers name,destination. QRForge previews the rows, renders one QR per row (up to 100 in this client-side version), and lets you download the PNGs or print the sheet. Bulk generation is not counted in the guest 10-free-codes limit.

Why are the first 10 codes free — and what happens at 10?

So you can try everything properly: as a guest you can create 10 codes free (a download or a save counts as one code). When you reach 10, we ask you to create a free account to continue — accounts are free and unlimited. It keeps the tool sustainable without putting a watermark on your codes or charging for downloads. Bulk CSV generation is not counted in the 10.

What is a tracked (dynamic) QR code?

Instead of encoding your destination directly, the QR encodes a short tracking link. Whoever scans it is forwarded to your destination, and the scan is recorded. Because the link stays the same, you can change the destination later in My Panel without reprinting, and you can see scan counts, platforms and rough locations. Static codes cannot do this — but they also never depend on any server.

Why does tracking need the free cloud connection?

A printed QR can only open a tracking link if something on the internet answers that link, forwards the visitor and writes the scan down — that "something" is a small free cloud backend (Supabase, free plan, no card). It is a one-time setup by the site owner; until it is switched on, tracked codes are registered in your panel with 0 scans and are clearly marked as not live yet. We label this honestly instead of pretending tracking works when it cannot.

What does a scan record — and what does it not record?

Each scan records only: the time, the platform and rough device type (for example iOS / Android / desktop, phone or tablet), and the rough country and city from the network connection. It does not record who the person is — no name, no email, no phone number, no exact address and no precise GPS location.

Where does my account live?

Until the free cloud connection is switched on, accounts are local: your name, email and a SHA-256 hash of your password (never the password itself) are stored only in this browser on this device, and your codes are kept per account on this device. That means your account works here, not yet on your phone or another computer — syncing across devices arrives with the same one-time cloud connection, which also switches on Google sign-in.

Which format should I download?

PNG for screens and most printing, JPG for photos/documents, SVG for professional print and signage (infinite scaling), and PDF for a ready-to-share one-page document.

About QRForge

QRForge is a free, browser-based QR code studio built for small businesses, restaurants, events and creators. It creates standards-based static QR codes for links, Wi-Fi, contact cards, events, menus, reviews and more — with logo embedding, brand colour sampling, contrast checking, a QR scanner and bulk generation from CSV.

Generation happens on your device, which is why QRForge works offline once loaded. Accounts and saved codes are stored locally in your browser on this device; the optional free cloud connection (Supabase) adds Google sign-in, cross-device accounts and scan tracking when the site owner switches it on.

Privacy Policy

QRForge does not collect, upload or store your QR content, logos, images or CSV files on any server — all processing happens locally in your browser.

Designs you save under “My Codes” are stored only in your own browser's localStorage and can be deleted at any time from that section or by clearing your browser data.

If you create an account, your name, email and a SHA-256 hash of your password (never the password itself) are stored only in this browser's localStorage on your device — nothing is sent to a server. If the owner has switched on the free cloud connection and you use tracked codes, a scan records only the time, platform/device type and rough country/city — no personal data about the person scanning.

The optional QR scanner fallback (jsQR) and the web font are loaded from public CDNs only when needed; if they cannot load, the core generator still works offline. Contact: hustlewithmuneeb@gmail.com.
